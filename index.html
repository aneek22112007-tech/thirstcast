30 Years of climatology

"Everyone forecasts how hot the air will be.
Nobody forecasts how thirsty it will be."

ThirstCast uses ET₀ — reference evapotranspiration — and 30 years of local historical thresholds to detect multi-day thirstwaves and translate them into estimated extra water demand, in litres.

The science

From weather to water demand

ET₀ represents atmospheric evaporative demand: how strongly weather conditions drive water loss from a reference surface. When ET₀ stays above its local seasonal 90th percentile for three or more days, a thirstwave has begun.

☁️ Atmosphere Temperature, humidity, wind, radiation
→
💧 ET₀ Evaporative demand, mm/day
→
📊 Local p90 1994–2023 day-of-year threshold
→
🌊 Thirstwave ≥3 consecutive days above p90

The definition

What is a thirstwave?

01

Heat, dry air, wind and sun

Together they set how fast water can leave a crop or a lake.

02

The sky pulls water

That pull is reference evapotranspiration, ET₀, in millimetres per day.

03

Three days above the local p90

A thirstwave is 3 or more consecutive days above the local day-of-year 90th percentile.

04

Litres estimated

Excess ET₀ is converted to extra irrigation need and surface-water evaporation loss.

The gap

Why it matters

Heat alerts say the air is hot. ThirstCast says how much faster water is leaving the surface — in litres — for a farmer, a lake manager, and a city planner.

Weighted for cropland area harvested, thirstwave intensity, duration, and frequency have increased by 0.06 mm d⁻¹ decade⁻¹, 0.10 days decade⁻¹, and 0.39 events decade⁻¹, respectively during 1981–2021. Statistically significant trends appear across 17%, 7%, and 23% of cropland area for intensity, frequency, and duration.

Kukal & Hobbins 2025, Earth's Future

This said, according to experts, there is essentially no data about extreme thirstwaves over India.

The Hindu, 24 June 2025
